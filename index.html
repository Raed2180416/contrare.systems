4.2 When a positive isn’t

Suppose 1 person in 100 has a certain condition. A screening test catches 90 of every 100 people who have it, and wrongly flags 5 of every 100 who don’t. Your result comes back positive. What are the chances you actually have it?
Most people say about ninety percent. The real answer is closer to fifteen. The puzzle dissolves once you take the seriously, and stop reading as though it were . The two are linked by , but they are different numbers answering different questions.

    Five promises, all the way down.

    1. The original is never rewritten.

      Your book stays your book. Everything Medes adds opens beneath the text, and one gesture closes it again.

    2. Every answer says where it came from.

      From your book General explanation Illustrative
      Each label reflects where the help actually came from. It isn’t decoration.

    3. When it can’t be sure, it shows you the page.

      An honest “not sure” beats a confident wrong answer. If something can’t be grounded, Medes says so and hands you back the original.

    4. Nothing opens on its own.

      No chat window lurking in the corner, and no streaks or badges. You pull, it answers, it folds away. The tutor offers a hint before it gives an answer.

    5. Your library lives with you.

      Books, notes and every layer you open are local files you own. When a model is used, it sees the idea you pulled, not your book.

    Built for two readers at once: the first-year who’s lost but curious, and the specialist who wants the assumptions, the notation, and room to disagree. They make the same gesture and aim it at different depths.
